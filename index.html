One iterative learning loop

Three Critical Phases of the Urban Design Workflow

The proposal-defined phases remain visible, but the learning experience is continuous: reveal the evidence gap, reconstruct missing conditions, ground synthetic evidence, interpret urban use, generate alternatives and re-audit the proposal.

Phase 1

Site Information Collection

GenAI reconstructs nighttime conditions that are incomplete in conventional site records, then exposes them to validation.

  1. Existing site and street-view data
  2. NightDiff nighttime synthesis
  3. Human perception validation
 → Phase 2

Predictive User Behavior Analysis

Comparative analysis examines observed space use and environmental mismatch, while predictive mapping estimates travel intensity and user-demand patterns from urban imagery.

  1. Compare nighttime-light representations
  2. Relate environmental measures to activity
  3. Diagnose spatial mismatch
 → Phase 3

Iterative Design Ideation

Parametric search and generative scenario testing produce alternatives that can be evaluated, compared and returned to the learning loop.

  1. Define objectives and constraints
  2. Generate morphology alternatives
  3. Evaluate, select and iterate
↶

Return path: evaluated alternatives become new visual and spatial conditions for observation, perception assessment and design refinement.

Transition from Phase 1 to Phase 2

From Perceived Environment to Observed Use of Urban Space

No single dataset explains how urban space works at night. Phase 2 combines complementary evidence while keeping their meanings distinct.

01

Perceived environment

Human ratings describe how a scene is visually interpreted.

+
02

Measured environment

Satellite nighttime light and street-level illumination describe different physical representations.

+
03

Observed urban activity

Nighttime mobile activity provides a spatial and temporal proxy for where people are present.

Interpretive boundary: brightness is not equivalent to safety, activity or social interaction. The teaching method asks students to compare these constructs, not collapse them into one measure.

Phase 2 · Student outcome and predictive extension

Predictive User Behavior Analysis

The selected student study compares environmental representations, examines observed activity and locates spatial mismatch without confusing association with causation. A separate predictive prototype complements this empirical reading by estimating patterns of space use and travel demand from satellite imagery.

Student Outcome · Nighttime urban audit

From environmental representation to human–space mismatch

The teaching case moves through three connected analyses so that each observation becomes the question for the next.

Research question

How do satellite and street-level nighttime-light representations differ?

Input
Satellite NTL and street-view-based NTL.
Method
Compare distributions and standardise values before cross-source analysis.
Learning outcome
Students recognise that sensing position and spatial resolution influence what is visible in an urban dataset.

Research question

How are environmental measures associated with observed nighttime activity?

Input
Standardised NTL measures and mobile-activity intensity.
Method
Test relationships across spatial scales and temporal windows.
Learning outcome
Students evaluate whether a representation of the environment aligns with observed use, without treating association as causation.

Research question

Where do representations disagree, and what built-environment conditions may contribute?

Mismatch is treated as a diagnostic signal. It directs attention to locations where top-down brightness and street-level illumination tell different stories, then supports closer examination of morphology, vegetation, population and lighting configuration.

Research Prototype · Predictive extension

Predict how people may use space from satellite imagery

The proposal defines user-behaviour prediction as a core Phase 2 capability. A separate case demonstrates how urban imagery can support estimates of travel intensity and bikeshare demand.

Predictive space-usage mapping

Make the user-demand prediction chain visible and testable

The prototype compares image-to-image learning approaches for translating satellite context into bike-demand surfaces. In teaching, students can inspect the input representation, training and test split, model comparison, spatial output and uncertainty.

Evidence boundary: the predictive prototype and the nighttime mismatch study are complementary teaching cases. They are not presented as one dataset or one empirical experiment.

Transition from Phase 2 to Phase 3

Translate Urban Diagnosis into Design Variables and Evaluation Criteria

Analysis becomes useful for design when observed conditions are translated into controllable variables, planning constraints and explicit performance objectives.

Diagnosis

Locate and interpret mismatch

Identify where representations and observed activity diverge; examine FAR, NDVI, population and LCZ gradients.

→
Design formulation

Define what can change

Select relevant variables such as height distribution, site coverage, setback, orientation, ground-floor interface, vegetation and lighting placement.

→
Design search

Generate and evaluate alternatives

Apply constraints, compare performance measures, select alternatives and refine the design.

Evidence boundary

The nighttime study identifies analytical questions and possible built-environment contributors. The MVI study below demonstrates the method of constrained morphology generation and evaluation. It is not presented as a direct empirical solution to nighttime-light mismatch.

Phase 3 · Student outcome and generative extension

Iterative Design Ideation

A selected student study turns design ideation into a traceable parametric search evaluated through Mountain View Index and Site Coverage Above Podium. The project prototype then extends the same logic from performance-led search to comparable morphology scenarios and environmental feedback.

Student Outcome · Performance-led morphology study

Translate a generative principle into a traceable design search

The MVI study demonstrates how students can define constraints, produce comparable alternatives and make evaluation consequential.

4Morphology familiesMixed, tower, courtyard and slab
400Design alternatives100 alternatives per family
6.0Fixed plot ratioConstant development intensity
0.10–0.30SCAP rangeSite Coverage Above Podium

Design sequence

Convert planning rules into a searchable design space

  1. Set constraints. Fix plot ratio and define an allowable SCAP range.
  2. Parameterise form. Vary morphology family, footprint, subdivision and orientation.
  3. Generate alternatives. Produce a comparable set for each morphology family.
  4. Retain traceability. Keep each alternative linked to its parameters and performance results.

Evaluation sequence

Compare performance before selecting and refining a form

  1. Measure performance. Evaluate Mountain View Index and SCAP for each alternative.
  2. Compare families. Examine distributions and morphology-specific tendencies.
  3. Select candidates. Identify alternatives that respond to the stated objective and constraints.
  4. Iterate. Adjust parameters and repeat the evaluation rather than selecting a single generated image.

Project Prototype Extension

From performance-led search to scenario testing

After students learn to generate and evaluate traceable alternatives, the prototype extends the same iterative principle: reconstruct selected morphology scenarios, compare environmental feedback and return the evidence to the next design decision.

Re-audit · Integrated teaching demonstration

Return design alternatives to the lived urban environment

The project-wide teaching loop can be closed by representing selected morphology alternatives under day and night scenarios, asking people to assess the resulting scenes, and comparing those responses with design-performance criteria. This is a proposed integration of the supplied outputs, not an already completed causal experiment.

  1. 01Select morphology
  2. 02Generate day/night views
  3. 03Collect perception ratings
  4. 04Compare performance
  5. 05Refine and repeat
